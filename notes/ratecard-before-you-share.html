The before-you-share checklist I use so a rate card still matches past invoices

A rate card can look quote-ready while its invoice story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the card leaves my hands.

A rate card can look quote-ready while its story is already wrong: phantom rates added so the Active column looks tidy; the same service from PDF + Stripe + Slack counted as three different prices; a silent scope upgrade greening a higher tier with no invoice crumb; a favor or pilot rate still marked as list price; rush premiums folded into base; a guessed “I usually charge somehow” locked as standard; a superseded old rate still traveling next to the real last charge; open asks scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff; or Slack “looks fine” folklore treated as a confirmed rate list.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-pass honesty cleanup. This is the pass I run when the card is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every Active row as what past invoices actually supported.

This complements earlier ratecard work (first-pass past-invoices-to-rate-card workflow and second-pass mid-rate honesty checklist). Those cover building and mid-pass honesty protection. This third pass is the pre-share evidence check — not a rewrite of either.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every Active rate maps to an invoice PDF, Stripe/export line, email quote, or a labeled ask — nothing invented for a tidy handoff preview.
  • Soft guesses (“I usually charge somehow,” “sounds like $X”) stay soft; hard current rates only appear when a source supports them or the owner explicitly chose the number.
  • Same-service duplicates across PDF + Stripe + Slack stay merged or labeled duplicate — they are not three open price points.
  • Silent scope upgrades into higher tiers are walked back or evidenced — they are not left greening a polished rate with no crumb.
  • Favor, nonprofit, pilot, or one-off rates still marked Active as list price are flipped to History / Exceptions — they do not still green this share’s Active list.
  • Rush, weekend, or travel premiums stay labeled premium — folding them into base without a note is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Guessed “standard” rates and “market somehow” numbers stay ask / not ready — they are not locked as fact because the column looked empty.
  • Superseded or zombie rates (older list price still looking live next to the real last charge) are labeled superseded / stale or removed from the share view.
  • Open asks are listed once — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • A Slack “looks fine,” emoji reaction, or vague “sounds good” is not treated as a locked rate list unless the card states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean rate card,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final card and live evidence: the original invoice dump (PDFs, Stripe / QuickBooks / Wave exports, email quotes, Notion half-tables, Slack fee DMs), prior rate card if any, and the mid-pass honesty scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the card becomes someone else’s quote reference for what was charged.

1. Freeze Active rates to what invoices actually supported

  • Every Active row maps to a source crumb, invoice id, export line, email quote, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “this is what I charge” look.
  • Phantom rates, template filler, and “bonus” services that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate rates under two (or three) phrasings — PDF + Stripe + Slack of the same charge — are merged or marked duplicate — one charge is not three open slots because it looked tidy three times.
  • Service wording stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same scope or the same unit.
  • Draft estimates and internal scratch stay out of Active — mixing them into one unlabeled “current rate” cell is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each Active row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented Active rate is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what past invoices already supported.

2. Keep last charged, current rate, unit, and status strength-honest

  • Soft notes (“I usually charge somehow,” “sounds like $150,” “probably day rate”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard current rates or units for neatness.
  • Hard current rate / unit / last charged keep an invoice line, export row, or confirmer when available — silence is not consent to a new list price.
  • Silent scope upgrades (row greened into a higher tier without a crumb — e.g. “landing page” quietly became “full site redesign”) are walked back to evidenced wording or labeled ask — they must not stay at a polished rate with no source.
  • Missing unit, last charged, current rate, or status stays ask / ghost — a blank cell that looks quote-ready is worse than a visible unknown.
  • Last charged and current rate stay separate — overwriting history so the card looks “clean” is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Estimated or unclear rows stay marked estimated — inventing exact numbers because the template looked empty is not cleanup.
  • Final skim: I can say which Active lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not pricing strategy or market advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding what anyone must charge next.

3. Separate Active from retired, favor/exception, premium, and open asks — list open asks once

  • Status vocabulary is named (active / seasonal / retired / exception / premium / ask) — not blank cells that look settled.
  • Evidence for retired, favor, or exception rates (note, archive flag, History section) sits in notes or source — “I think we stopped offering that” alone is not retired.
  • Favor, nonprofit, pilot, or one-off rates still marked Active as list price are flipped off Active — they do not still green this share’s quote list because the PDF is still in the dump.
  • Rush / weekend / travel premiums stay labeled premium or sit in Exceptions — burial in a notes cell is not a status.
  • Tax-inclusive vs tax-exclusive amounts stay flagged — mixing them silently is folklore, not a rate.
  • Open asks (missing PDF, unclear unit, which phrasing is canonical, conflict between last charged and sticky “usual,” tax treatment unclear) are listed once in a visible place — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • Final skim: I can say what is Active, what is retired/exception/premium, and what still needs confirmation without reopening Gmail.

A favor-rate ghost can be useful History context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s list price.

4. Retire zombie and stale rate versions before they travel

  • Superseded rates (older list price still looking live next to the real last charge, or an outdated sticky next to a later invoice) are labeled superseded or removed from the share view — they do not still inflate Active.
  • Walked-back, withdrawn, wrong-service, or wrong-currency rows are labeled stale or stay off — they do not still green the Active list.
  • When PDF, Stripe, and Slack disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier number because it fits the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material Active rows — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected averages, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Final skim: I can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older rate version, or something still needing confirmation.

A zombie superseded rate is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s current asking rate.

5. Keep Slack and reactions from rewriting the rate list

  • Replace “looks fine” / “sounds good” folklore with the exact services, units, last-charged amounts, current rates, statuses, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the card.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite rates, units, Active/retired flags, or favor-as-list-price rows.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the card you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this service wording, this unit, this last charged, this current rate, this retired flag, this superseded rate — instead of asking for another vague “looks fine.”
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the rate card look complete by guessing.
