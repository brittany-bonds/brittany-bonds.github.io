The before-you-share checklist I use so a weekly priorities one-pager still matches what was open

A weekly priorities one-pager can look Monday-ready while its week story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the page leaves my hands.

A weekly priorities one-pager can look Monday-ready while its week story is already wrong: a phantom Top-3 sits in the page for handoff polish; capacity is invented so the plan looks doable; two conflicting deadlines are averaged into one tidy spine; a Slack “sounds good” is greened as a locked priority set; a stale backlog is still shipping as this week; or a soft “maybe this week” is upgraded to a done-definition priority with no dump support.

Before-you-share is different from mid-week cleanup. This is the pass I run when the one-pager is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every line as what was actually open and locked for the week.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every Top 3–5 item maps to something in the dump (or a labeled ask) — nothing invented for completeness.
  • Soft “maybe this week” stays soft; locked priority language only appears when a source supports it.
  • Conflicting deadlines stay labeled conflict / ask — they are not averaged into one green week spine.
  • Slack guesses and “I think we decided…” stay labeled guess / ask — not client-confirmed.
  • Superseded or out-of-window backlog is labeled stale or removed from the share view.
  • An emoji reaction or vague “sounds good” is not treated as a locked priority set unless the page states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean week plan,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final one-pager and live evidence: the original task dump, sticky photos, Slack/email exports, Notion/Todoist dumps, calendar holds, capacity notes, prior week page if any, and the mid-week scratch trail. The goal is not to make every line complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the page becomes someone else’s Monday source of truth about what was open.

1. Freeze Top 3–5 to what was open

  • Every priority maps to a dump line, screenshot, thread, or a labeled ask — no orphan line gets a confident “this is the week” look.
  • Invented Top-3 items, template filler, and “bonus” priorities that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate asks under two phrasings (Slack + sticky of the same task) are merged or marked duplicate — one ask is not two priority slots because it looked tidy twice.
  • Done-definition wording stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same deliverable or client.
  • Soft “maybe this week” noise stays soft or parked — upgrading it to a locked done-definition without a dump crumb is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each Top item back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented priority is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what was already open for the week.

2. Keep capacity and week spine strength-honest

  • Soft capacity notes (“busy week,” “half day Thursday”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to a hard hour budget for neatness.
  • Hard deadlines keep a source, date, or named confirmer when available — silence is not consent.
  • Hours, energy, and travel assumptions stay tied to a source or labeled guess / TBD / ask.
  • Competing week windows stay side by side as conflict / ask — do not average two calendars into one green spine.
  • Week language matches the latest source; a polished synonym may not be the same Mon–Sun.
  • Invented capacity so the page “looks doable” stays out or labeled guess / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Skim test: a reader can tell which lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not productivity coaching. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding what anyone must do this week.

3. Retire stale backlog before it travels

  • Walked-back, canceled, or out-of-window backlog is labeled stale or removed from the share view — it does not still green a Monday cell.
  • When Slack, sticky, and Notion disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier Top-3 because it fits the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material priorities and deadlines — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected options, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Skim test: a reader can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older backlog, or something still needing confirmation.

A stale backlog item is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this week’s share-ready priority.

4. Challenge the Slack “sounds good”

  • Replace “they said sounds good” with the exact priorities that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the one-pager.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite Top-3 or capacity totals.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the page you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this priority, this deadline, this conflict — instead of asking for another vague thumbs-up.
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the week plan look complete by guessing.
