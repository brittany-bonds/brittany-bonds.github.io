The before-you-share checklist I use so a reimbursement claim sheet still matches what was spent

A reimbursement claim sheet can look finished while its expense story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the sheet leaves my hands.

A reimbursement claim sheet can look finished while its expense story is already wrong: a line nobody spent sits in the sheet for polish; a soft receipt is dressed as a hard category; two conflicting amounts share one tidy total; a Slack guess is greened as client-confirmed; a superseded receipt is still shipping; or an emoji “sounds good” is doing all the approving with no matching line totals.

Before-you-share is different from mid-expense cleanup. This is the pass I run when the sheet is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every line as what was actually spent and locked.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every claim line maps to something in the dump (or a labeled ask) — nothing invented for completeness.
  • Soft receipts stay soft; hard category language only appears when a source supports it.
  • Conflicting amounts stay labeled conflict / ask — they are not averaged into one green total.
  • Slack guesses and “I think they meant…” stay labeled guess / ask — not client-confirmed.
  • Superseded or replaced receipts are labeled stale or removed from the share view.
  • An emoji reaction or vague “sounds good” is not treated as locked totals unless the sheet states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean claim,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final claim sheet and live evidence: the original expense dump, receipt photos/PDFs, card/CSV export, email thread, Slack, prior claim if any, and the mid-expense scratch trail. The goal is not to make every line complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the sheet becomes someone else’s source of truth about what was spent.

1. Freeze lines to what was spent

  • Every claim line maps to a receipt, card charge, email forward, or a labeled ask — no orphan line gets a confident “they wanted this covered” look.
  • Invented line items, template filler, and “bonus” expenses that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate charges under two phrasings (email + photo of the same lunch) are merged or marked duplicate — one spend is not two lines because it looked tidy twice.
  • Payee and purpose wording stay close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same expense.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each line back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented line item is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what was already spent.

2. Keep receipt and category strength-honest

  • Soft receipts (blurry photo, partial PDF, “I’ll find it later”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard category certainty for neatness.
  • Hard categories keep a source, date, or named confirmer when available — silence is not consent.
  • Amounts, currencies, and conversions stay tied to a source or labeled guess / TBD / ask.
  • Competing amounts stay side by side as conflict / ask — do not average two figures into one green total.
  • Category language matches the latest source; a polished synonym may not be the same bucket.
  • Skim test: a reader can tell which lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not reimbursement-policy advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding what anyone must claim or approve.

3. Retire superseded receipts before they travel

  • Walked-back, replaced, or older receipts are labeled stale or removed from the share view — they do not still green a claim cell.
  • When email, Slack, and card export disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier amount because it fits the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material lines — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected options, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Skim test: a reader can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older receipt, or something still needing confirmation.

A superseded receipt is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as today’s claim-ready spend.

4. Challenge the Slack “sounds good”

  • Replace “they said sounds good” with the exact points that were confirmed, open, or never named on the claim sheet.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite amounts or category totals.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the sheet you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this line, this amount, this conflict — instead of asking for another vague thumbs-up.
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the claim look complete by guessing.
