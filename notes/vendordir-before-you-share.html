The before-you-share checklist I use so a vendor directory still matches the contact dump

A vendor directory can look share-ready while its contact story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the sheet leaves my hands.

A vendor directory can look share-ready while its story is already wrong: invented emails or phones padded so the Active contact column looks full; the same supplier under two (or three) spellings treated as twin Preferred rows; a silent stale contact (last year’s business-card photo greened as today’s truth with no last-checked date); payment-term ghosts (“Net 30” with no crumb) looking buy-ready; W-9 / tax-status folklore (“they’re set up”) upgraded to hard status with no file crumb; do-not-use / superseded vendors deleted so the old remit-to disappears from memory; a zombie superseded contact still looking live next to a newer account; open asks scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff; Slack “looks fine” / emoji folklore treated as locked contacts; or soft guesses (“Mike handles bolts,” “usually fast”) upgraded to hard terms with no crumb.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-reorder honesty cleanup. This is the pass I run when the sheet is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every Active vendor line as what the contact dump actually supported.

This complements earlier vendordir work (first-pass vendor-contacts-to-directory workflow and second-pass mid-reorder honesty checklist). Those cover building and mid-pass honesty protection. This third pass is the pre-share evidence check — not a rewrite of either.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every Active vendor line maps to a contact crumb — email export, invoice header, business-card photo, text screenshot, portal bookmark, sticky note, old spreadsheet leftover, or a labeled ask — nothing invented for a tidy handoff preview.
  • Soft guesses (“Mike handles bolts,” “usually Net 30,” “portal still works”) stay soft; hard phones, emails, terms, and statuses only appear when a source supports them or the owner explicitly chose them.
  • Same-vendor duplicates under two spellings stay merged or labeled duplicate — they are not two open Preferred rows.
  • Silent stale contacts (old card photo greened as today’s truth with no last-checked date) are walked back or evidenced — they are not left polishing a phone with no freshness crumb.
  • Payment terms, lead time, and preferred channel stay named or flagged — blank “looks buy-ready” cells are worse than a visible unknown.
  • W-9 / tax-status folklore stays folklore unless a file crumb or labeled ask exists — “they’re set up” is not a shareable tax status.
  • Do-not-use and superseded vendors stay visible or labeled do not use / superseded — deleting them so the old remit-to disappears from memory is not honesty.
  • Superseded or zombie contacts (older account still looking live next to a newer remit-to) are labeled superseded / stale or removed from the share view.
  • Open asks are listed once — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • A Slack “looks fine,” emoji reaction, or vague “sounds good” is not treated as locked contacts unless the sheet states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean vendor directory,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final sheet and live evidence: the original vendor-contact dump (email exports, text screenshots, business-card photos, invoice headers, portal bookmarks, sticky “ask Mike” notes, old CRM/CSV scraps), prior directory if any, and the mid-reorder honesty scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the sheet becomes someone else’s buy list for who to call, email, or pay.

1. Freeze Active vendors to what the contact dump actually supported

  • Every Active row maps to a source crumb, email export, invoice header, card photo, text screenshot, portal bookmark, sticky, spreadsheet leftover, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “order from this today” look.
  • Invented emails, phones, websites, template filler, and “bonus” vendors that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate vendors under two (or three) phrasings — “Acme Supply,” “ACME Supplies LLC,” and a sticky “Acme bolts” — are merged or marked duplicate — one company is not two open Preferred slots.
  • Legal / trade name wording stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same remit-to or the same LLC.
  • Draft estimates, internal scratch, and unsent “we’ll confirm later” drafts stay out of Active — mixing them into one unlabeled “contact” cell is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each Active row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented Active vendor line is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what the contact dump already supported.

2. Keep phones, emails, terms, lead times, and W-9 folklore strength-honest

  • Soft notes (“Mike handles bolts,” “usually fast,” “I think Net 30”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard phone / email / terms for neatness with no crumb.
  • Hard phone / email / website / payment terms / lead time keep a crumb or confirmer when available — silence is not consent to a new buy plan.
  • Silent stale contacts (row greened from last year’s card photo into “today’s” Active without a last-checked date) are walked back to evidenced wording or labeled ask / stale — they must not stay at a polished phone with no freshness crumb.
  • Payment-term ghosts (“Net 30” with no invoice crumb) stay flagged or labeled ask — a blank terms cell that looks buy-ready is worse than a visible unknown.
  • W-9 / tax-status folklore (“they’re set up,” “we have their W-9 somewhere”) stays folklore or labeled ask — inventing a hard tax-ready status with no file crumb is not cleanup, and I am not giving tax advice.
  • Missing phone, email, category, terms, lead time, account note, or preferred channel stays ask / ghost — a blank cell that looks order-ready is worse than a visible unknown.
  • Estimated or unclear rows stay marked estimated — inventing exact terms because the template looked empty is not cleanup.
  • Final skim: I can say which Active lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not purchasing strategy, tax, or vendor-compliance advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding who anyone must buy from or treat as tax-ready.

3. Separate Active from stale, do-not-use/superseded, and open asks — list open asks once

  • Status vocabulary is named (active / preferred / seasonal / one-off / do not use / superseded / stale / soft / needs confirm / ask) — not blank cells that look settled.
  • Evidence for stale, soft, do-not-use, or parked contacts (note, archive flag, Soft / History section) sits in notes or source — “I think we stopped using them” alone is not closed.
  • Do-not-use and superseded vendors stay visible or labeled do not use / superseded — they do not get deleted so the old remit-to disappears from memory because the tidy share looked cleaner without them.
  • Category vs contact vs remit-to vs portal stays labeled — burial of type in a notes cell is not a status.
  • Fake certainty (“exact Net 30” / “W-9 on file” with no crumb) stays flagged — mixing it into true confirmed terms without a note is folklore, not a contact.
  • Open asks (missing last-checked date, unclear remit-to, which spelling is canonical, conflict between invoice and sticky, which twin is Preferred, confidential strip needed) are listed once in a visible place — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • Final skim: I can say what is Active, what is stale/soft/do-not-use/superseded, and what still needs confirmation without reopening Slack.

A soft “Mike handles bolts” ghost can be useful Soft context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s real Active preferred contact.

4. Retire zombie and superseded contacts before they travel

  • Superseded contacts (older account still looking live next to a newer remit-to, later invoice header, or reconciled portal) are labeled superseded or removed from the share view — they do not still inflate Active Preferred.
  • Walked-back, withdrawn, wrong-LLC, or already-replaced rows are labeled stale or stay off — they do not still green the Active list.
  • When invoice header, email signature, and sticky disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier spelling because it fits the template, and do not wrongly keep twin Preferred rows because the sheet looked sparse.
  • Last-checked or last-order date exists for material Active rows — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected merges, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Final skim: I can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older contact version, or something still needing confirmation.

A zombie superseded contact is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s current Active vendor.

5. Keep Slack and reactions from rewriting the buy list

  • Replace “looks fine” / “sounds good” folklore with the exact vendors, phones, emails, terms, lead times, statuses, remit-tos, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the sheet.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite contacts, terms, Active/stale flags, twin Preferred merges, W-9 folklore, or deleted do-not-use rows.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the sheet you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this vendor, this phone, this email, this remit-to, this payment terms crumb, this superseded contact — instead of asking for another vague “looks fine.”
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the vendor directory look complete by guessing.
