The before-you-share checklist I use so a mileage log still matches what was driven

A mileage log can look finished while its trip story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the sheet leaves my hands.

A mileage log can look finished while its trip story is already wrong: a trip nobody drove sits in the sheet for polish; a soft note is dressed as locked miles; two conflicting odometer, GPS, or app totals share one tidy figure; a Slack guess is greened as client-confirmed; a superseded trip is still shipping; or an emoji “sounds good” is doing all the approving with no matching mile totals.

Before-you-share is different from mid-trip cleanup. This is the pass I run when the sheet is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every row as what was actually driven and locked.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every trip row maps to something in the dump (or a labeled ask) — nothing invented for completeness.
  • Soft notes stay soft; locked-mile language only appears when a source supports it.
  • Conflicting odometer / GPS / app totals stay labeled conflict / ask — they are not averaged into one green total.
  • Slack guesses and “I think they meant…” stay labeled guess / ask — not client-confirmed.
  • Superseded or replaced trips are labeled stale or removed from the share view.
  • An emoji reaction or vague “sounds good” is not treated as locked miles unless the sheet states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean mileage log,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final mileage log and live evidence: the original trip dump, odometer photos, GPS/app export, calendar, email thread, Slack, prior log if any, and the mid-trip scratch trail. The goal is not to make every row complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the sheet becomes someone else’s source of truth about what was driven.

1. Freeze trips to what was driven

  • Every trip row maps to an odometer reading, GPS/app export, calendar entry, email forward, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “they wanted this trip covered” look.
  • Invented trips, template filler, and “bonus” miles that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate trips under two phrasings (calendar + app of the same drive) are merged or marked duplicate — one drive is not two rows because it looked tidy twice.
  • Origin, destination, and purpose wording stay close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same trip.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented trip is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what was already driven.

2. Keep soft notes and mile totals strength-honest

  • Soft notes (blurry odometer photo, partial GPS export, “I’ll find the app later”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to locked-mile certainty for neatness.
  • Locked miles keep a source, date, or named confirmer when available — silence is not consent.
  • Mile totals, round-trips, and unit conversions stay tied to a source or labeled guess / TBD / ask.
  • Competing odometer, GPS, and app totals stay side by side as conflict / ask — do not average two figures into one green total.
  • Purpose / business-vs-personal language matches the latest source; a polished synonym may not be the same bucket.
  • Skim test: a reader can tell which rows are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not mileage-rate or reimbursement-policy advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding what anyone must claim or approve.

3. Retire superseded trips before they travel

  • Walked-back, replaced, or older trips are labeled stale or removed from the share view — they do not still green a mileage cell.
  • When email, Slack, odometer, and app export disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier miles because they fit the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material rows — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected options, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Skim test: a reader can tell whether each row describes the current dump, an older trip, or something still needing confirmation.

A superseded trip is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as today’s share-ready miles.

4. Challenge the Slack “sounds good”

  • Replace “they said sounds good” with the exact points that were confirmed, open, or never named on the mileage log.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite mile totals or trip purpose.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the sheet you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this trip, these miles, this conflict — instead of asking for another vague thumbs-up.
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this row, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready trip.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the mileage log look complete by guessing.
