The before-you-share checklist I use so a remaining-hours tracker still matches what is live

A retainer tracker can look renew-ready while its hours-dump story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the sheet leaves my hands.

A remaining-hours tracker can look renew-ready while its story is already wrong: phantom hours added so the open balance looks tidy; the same retainer from sticky + Slack + invoice PDF counted three times; a silent remaining-balance upgrade greening “plenty left” with no evidence; an expired period still marked active; project hours mixed into retainer used; a guessed “about 8 left somehow” locked as remaining fact; a superseded sticky still traveling next to the real budget; open asks scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff; or Slack “looks fine” folklore treated as a confirmed renew-and-invoice list.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-period cleanup. This is the pass I run when the sheet is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every active row as what is still left to renew or invoice from.

This complements earlier retainersheet work (first-pass retainer-notes-to-tracker workflow and mid-period remaining-hours honesty cleanup). Those cover building and mid-pass cleanup. This third pass is the pre-share evidence check — not a rewrite of either.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every active row maps to a sticky/Slack/invoice/time-log crumb, period note, or a labeled ask — nothing invented for a tidy renew-and-invoice preview.
  • Soft guesses (“about 8 left somehow,” “usual rollover”) stay soft; hard remaining and renewal dates only appear when a source supports them.
  • Same-retainer duplicates across sticky + Slack + invoice PDF stay merged or labeled duplicate — they are not three open retainers.
  • Silent remaining-balance upgrades are walked back or evidenced — they are not left greening “plenty left” with no crumb.
  • Expired periods still marked active are flipped off Active — they do not still green this window’s renew slice.
  • Project hours and retainer hours stay separated — mixing them into one unlabeled “used” cell is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Guessed remaining stays ask / guess — it is not locked as renew fact because the column looked empty.
  • Superseded or zombie notes (older sticky still looking live next to the real budget) are labeled superseded / stale or removed from the share view.
  • Open asks are listed once — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • A Slack “looks fine,” emoji reaction, or vague “renew when you can” is not treated as a locked renew list unless the sheet states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean remaining-hours tracker,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final sheet and live evidence: the original retainer hours dump (notes app, Slack threads, calendar blocks, invoice PDFs, time-log exports, sticky “~8 hrs left??” scraps), prior active-retainer sheet if any, and the mid-period scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the sheet becomes someone else’s renew-and-invoice source of truth.

1. Freeze active rows to what is still live

  • Every active row maps to a source crumb, screenshot, export, sticky, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “this is the renew list” look.
  • Phantom hours, template filler, and “bonus” retainers that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate retainers under two (or three) phrasings — sticky + Slack + invoice PDF of the same client + period — are merged or marked duplicate — one retainer is not three open slots because it looked tidy three times.
  • Client naming stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same engagement or the same period.
  • Project hours stay out of retainer used — mixing them into one unlabeled column is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each active row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented active row is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what is already live for this renew window.

2. Keep budget, used, remaining, and date strength-honest

  • Soft notes (“about 8 left somehow,” “usual rollover,” “probably fine through Friday”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard remaining or renewal dates for neatness.
  • Hard budget / used / remaining keep an invoice line, time-log export, calendar block, date, or confirmer when available — silence is not consent to “plenty left.”
  • Silent remaining-balance upgrades (row greened without a crumb) are walked back to evidenced remaining or labeled ask — they must not stay at a polished balance with no source.
  • Missing budget, used, remaining, or period end stays ask / ghost — a blank cell that looks renew-ready is worse than a visible unknown.
  • Guessed remaining and “about Friday somehow” renew dates stay labeled guess / ask — converting vibes into a tidy renew-this-week slice without a note is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Estimated rows stay marked estimated — inventing exact remaining because the template looked empty is not cleanup.
  • Final skim: I can say which active lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not billing strategy or contract advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding what anyone must renew or invoice.

3. Separate renew-ready from expired ghosts, overage parking, and closed periods — list open asks once

  • Status vocabulary is named (active / expired-still-open / closed / overage / ask) — not blank cells that look renew-ready.
  • Evidence for expired, closed, or rolled-over periods (invoice, note, archive flag) sits in notes or source — “I think that period ended” alone is not closed.
  • Expired periods still marked active are flipped off Active — they do not still green this window’s renew slice because the sticky is still in the dump.
  • Overage / change parking stays out of remaining-hours filters until status says otherwise — burial in a thread is not a status.
  • Rollover vs use-it-or-lose-it is stated once per retainer — folklore that “they always roll” is not a rule.
  • Open asks (missing budget, unclear period end, which client spelling is canonical, who owns renew, conflicting sticky vs invoice) are listed once in a visible place — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • Final skim: I can say what is renew-ready, what is expired/closed, what is overage/parked, and what still needs confirmation without reopening Slack.

An expired ghost can be useful archive context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this window’s share-ready active retainer.

4. Retire zombie and stale note versions before they travel

  • Superseded notes (older sticky still looking live next to the real budget, or an outdated invoice PDF next to a later time-log) are labeled superseded or removed from the share view — they do not still inflate Active remaining.
  • Walked-back, withdrawn, out-of-window, or wrong-client rows are labeled stale or stay off — they do not still green the renew list.
  • When sticky, Slack, and invoice disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier remaining or renew date because it fits the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material active rows — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected options, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Final skim: I can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older note version, or something still needing confirmation.

A zombie superseded note is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this window’s share-ready remaining balance.

5. Keep Slack and reactions from rewriting the renew list

  • Replace “looks fine” / “renew when you can” folklore with the exact clients, budgets, remaining, renew dates, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the sheet.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite remaining, renew dates, or expired/active flags.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the sheet you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this client, this budget, this remaining, this renew date, this expired flag, this superseded note — instead of asking for another vague “looks fine.”
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the remaining-hours tracker look complete by guessing.
