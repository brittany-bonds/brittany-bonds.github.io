The before-you-share checklist I use so an inventory sheet still matches the stock-count dump

An inventory sheet can look share-ready while its on-hand story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the sheet leaves my hands.

An inventory sheet can look share-ready while its story is already wrong: invented on-hand quantities padded so the Active count column looks full; the same SKU counted twice across clipboard + spreadsheet + photo and treated as two rows or wrongly summed; a silent stale count (old spreadsheet greened as today’s truth with no count date); unit mixing (12 eaches next to 12 boxes with no unit) looking share-ready; variants (size / color / pack) collapsed into one blob so the share lies about which is low; zero-qty / discontinued rows deleted so the SKU disappears from reorder memory; a zombie superseded count still looking live next to a newer recount; open asks scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff; Slack “looks fine” / emoji folklore treated as locked counts; or soft guesses (“about 20”) upgraded to hard qty with no crumb.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-count honesty cleanup. This is the pass I run when the sheet is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every Active on-hand line as what the stock-count dump actually supported.

This complements earlier inventorysheet work (first-pass stock-counts-to-inventory-sheet workflow and second-pass mid-count honesty checklist). Those cover building and mid-pass honesty protection. This third pass is the pre-share evidence check — not a rewrite of either.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every Active on-hand line maps to a count crumb — clipboard photo, shelf photo, handwritten tally, POS screenshot, packing list, spreadsheet leftover, or a labeled ask — nothing invented for a tidy handoff preview.
  • Soft guesses (“about 20,” “looks like a full bin,” “wire ~40 left”) stay soft; hard quantities only appear when a source supports them or the owner explicitly chose them.
  • Same-SKU duplicates across clipboard + spreadsheet + photo stay merged or labeled duplicate — they are not two open on-hand rows or a silently summed total.
  • Silent stale counts (old spreadsheet greened as today’s truth with no count date) are walked back or evidenced — they are not left polishing a qty with no freshness crumb.
  • Units stay named (each / box / case / roll / lb) — 12 eaches next to 12 boxes with no unit is not share-ready.
  • Variants (size / color / pack) stay on separate rows — collapsing them into one “assortment” blob so the share lies about which is low is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Zero-qty and discontinued rows stay visible or labeled out / discontinued — deleting them so the SKU disappears from reorder memory is not honesty.
  • Superseded or zombie counts (older count still looking live next to a newer recount) are labeled superseded / stale or removed from the share view.
  • Open asks are listed once — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • A Slack “looks fine,” emoji reaction, or vague “sounds good” is not treated as locked counts unless the sheet states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean inventory sheet,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final sheet and live evidence: the original stock-count dump (handwritten tallies, phone photos of shelves/bins, old spreadsheet exports, POS screenshots, supplier packing lists, sticky “~40 left” notes), prior inventory sheet if any, and the mid-count honesty scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the sheet becomes someone else’s stock plan for what to reorder or book as on hand.

1. Freeze Active on-hand to what the stock-count dump actually supported

  • Every Active row maps to a source crumb, clipboard photo, shelf photo, tally, POS screenshot, packing list, spreadsheet leftover, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “reorder from this today” look.
  • Invented on-hand quantities, template filler, and “bonus” stock that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate SKUs under two (or three) phrasings — clipboard + spreadsheet + photo of the same count — are merged or marked duplicate — one SKU is not two open on-hand slots, and it is not silently summed because it looked tidy three times.
  • SKU / item / location wording stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same SKU or the same bin.
  • Draft estimates, internal scratch, and unsent “we’ll recount later” drafts stay out of Active on-hand — mixing them into one unlabeled “qty” cell is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each Active row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented Active on-hand line is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what the stock-count dump already supported.

2. Keep quantities, units, variants, and count dates strength-honest

  • Soft notes (“about 20,” “looks like a full bin,” “wire ~40 left”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard qty for neatness with no crumb.
  • Hard qty / unit / location / count date keep a crumb or confirmer when available — silence is not consent to a new stock plan.
  • Silent stale counts (row greened from an old spreadsheet into “today’s” on-hand without a count date) are walked back to evidenced wording or labeled ask / stale — they must not stay at a polished qty with no freshness crumb.
  • Unit mixing (12 eaches next to 12 boxes with no unit) stays flagged or split — a blank unit cell that looks share-ready is worse than a visible unknown.
  • Variants (size / color / pack) stay on separate rows — collapsing them into one blob so the share lies about which is low is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Missing qty, unit, location, count date, SKU ID, or reorder flag stays ask / ghost — a blank cell that looks count-ready is worse than a visible unknown.
  • Estimated or unclear rows stay marked estimated — inventing exact qty because the template looked empty is not cleanup.
  • Final skim: I can say which Active lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not inventory-control strategy or ERP advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding what anyone must reorder next.

3. Separate Active from stale, zero/out/discontinued, and open asks — list open asks once

  • Status vocabulary is named (active / estimated / counted / low / out / discontinued / stale / soft / needs ID / ask) — not blank cells that look settled.
  • Evidence for stale, soft, discontinued, or parked counts (note, archive flag, Soft / History section) sits in notes or source — “I think we stopped stocking that” alone is not closed.
  • Zero-qty and discontinued rows stay visible or labeled out / discontinued — they do not get deleted so the SKU disappears from reorder memory because the tidy share looked cleaner without them.
  • Location vs unit vs variant stays labeled — burial of type in a notes cell is not a status.
  • Fake certainty (“exact 20” with no count crumb) stays flagged — mixing it into true counted qty without a note is folklore, not a count.
  • Open asks (missing count date, unclear unit, which source is canonical, conflict between clipboard and spreadsheet, which variant is low, confidential strip needed) are listed once in a visible place — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • Final skim: I can say what is Active, what is stale/soft/out/discontinued, and what still needs confirmation without reopening Slack.

A soft “about 20” ghost can be useful Soft context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s real Active counted qty.

4. Retire zombie and superseded counts before they travel

  • Superseded counts (older count still looking live next to a newer recount, later photo, or reconciled spreadsheet) are labeled superseded or removed from the share view — they do not still inflate Active on-hand.
  • Walked-back, withdrawn, wrong-location, or already-reconciled rows are labeled stale or stay off — they do not still green the Active list.
  • When clipboard, spreadsheet, and photo disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier number because it fits the template, and do not wrongly sum them into one “tidy” qty.
  • Count date or last-checked date exists for material Active rows — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected merges, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Final skim: I can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older count version, or something still needing confirmation.

A zombie superseded count is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s current Active on-hand.

5. Keep Slack and reactions from rewriting the stock plan

  • Replace “looks fine” / “sounds good” folklore with the exact SKUs, quantities, units, locations, count dates, variants, statuses, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the sheet.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite qty, units, Active/stale flags, collapsed variants, or deleted zero-qty rows.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the sheet you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this SKU, this qty, this unit, this location, this count date, this superseded count — instead of asking for another vague “looks fine.”
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the inventory sheet look complete by guessing.
