The before-you-share checklist I use so a job-cost sheet still matches hours + materials

A job-cost sheet can look share-ready while its hours-and-materials story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the sheet leaves my hands.

A job-cost sheet can look share-ready while its story is already wrong: invented billable hours padded so the Active cost column looks full; the same material counted twice across receipts + spreadsheet + Slack; a silent upgrade turning an estimate into an actual with no crumb; a zombie superseded cost dump still looking live next to a real receipt; open asks scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff; Slack “looks fine” / emoji folklore treated as locked costs; or favor / non-billable hours still marked Active as real job cost.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-job honesty cleanup. This is the pass I run when the sheet is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every Active cost line as what the hours + materials dump actually supported.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every Active cost line maps to a timesheet crumb, receipt, invoice stub, Slack DM, spreadsheet leftover, calendar ghost, or a labeled ask — nothing invented for a tidy handoff preview.
  • Soft guesses (“probably about four hours,” “materials around that”) stay soft; hard hours, quantities, and dollar actuals only appear when a source supports them or the owner explicitly chose them.
  • Same-material duplicates across receipts + spreadsheet + Slack stay merged or labeled duplicate — they are not three open cost lines.
  • Silent estimate-to-actual upgrades (estimate greened into actual with no crumb) are walked back or evidenced — they are not left polishing a cost with no source.
  • Favor, non-billable, or one-off “I’ll absorb this” hours still marked Active as real job cost are flipped to Soft / Non-billable / Parked — they do not still green this share’s Active cost list.
  • Hours vs materials vs pass-through stays labeled when the column mixes them — folding soft favor hours into billable materials without a note is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Guessed “enough hours” and “materials somehow” numbers stay ask / estimated — they are not locked as fact because the column looked empty.
  • Superseded or zombie cost dumps (older estimate still looking live next to a real receipt or later actual) are labeled superseded / stale or removed from the share view.
  • Open asks are listed once — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • A Slack “looks fine,” emoji reaction, or vague “sounds good” is not treated as locked costs unless the sheet states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean job-cost sheet,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final sheet and live evidence: the original hours + materials dump (timesheets, receipt photos, bank CSV lines, Slack “bought the parts” DMs, spreadsheet leftovers, calendar ghosts, Notion half-checkboxes), prior job-cost sheet if any, and the mid-job honesty scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the sheet becomes someone else’s cost plan for what to bill or book.

1. Freeze Active costs to what hours + materials actually supported

  • Every Active row maps to a source crumb, timesheet, receipt, invoice stub, Slack DM, spreadsheet leftover, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “bill this today” look.
  • Invented billable hours, template filler, and “bonus” materials that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate materials under two (or three) phrasings — receipt + spreadsheet + Slack of the same purchase — are merged or marked duplicate — one material is not three open cost slots because it looked tidy three times.
  • Job, task, and material wording stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same job or the same spend.
  • Draft estimates, internal scratch, and unsent “we’ll bill later” drafts stay out of Active actuals — mixing them into one unlabeled “job cost” cell is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each Active row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented Active cost line is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what the hours + materials dump already supported.

2. Keep hours, quantities, rates, and actuals strength-honest

  • Soft notes (“probably about four hours,” “materials around that,” “sounds billable”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard hours, quantities, or dollar actuals for neatness.
  • Hard hours / quantity / rate / actual keep a crumb or confirmer when available — silence is not consent to a new cost plan.
  • Silent estimate-to-actual upgrades (row greened from estimate to actual without a timesheet or receipt crumb) are walked back to evidenced wording or labeled ask — they must not stay at a polished actual with no source.
  • Missing hours, quantity, rate, vendor, job tag, or billable flag stays ask / ghost — a blank cell that looks bill-ready is worse than a visible unknown.
  • Estimate and actual stay separate — overwriting history so the sheet looks “clean” is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Estimated or unclear rows stay marked estimated — inventing exact hours or dollar actuals because the template looked empty is not cleanup.
  • Final skim: I can say which Active lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not pricing strategy or billing advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding what anyone must charge next.

3. Separate Active from stale, soft/favor, non-billable, and open asks — list open asks once

  • Status vocabulary is named (active / estimated / actual / non-billable / favor / stale / soft / done / cancelled / ask) — not blank cells that look settled.
  • Evidence for stale, soft, favor, or parked costs (note, archive flag, Soft / History section) sits in notes or source — “I think we stopped counting that” alone is not closed.
  • Favor, non-billable, or one-off “I’ll absorb this” hours still marked Active as real job cost are flipped off Active — they do not still green this share’s cost list because the sticky is still in the dump.
  • Hours vs materials vs pass-through stays labeled — burial of type in a notes cell is not a status.
  • Fake certainty (“exact hours” with no timesheet) stays flagged — mixing it into true actuals without a note is folklore, not a cost.
  • Open asks (missing hours crumb, unclear quantity, which receipt is canonical, conflict between spreadsheet and Slack, confidential strip needed) are listed once in a visible place — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • Final skim: I can say what is Active, what is stale/soft/favor/non-billable, and what still needs confirmation without reopening Slack.

A soft “absorb this” ghost can be useful Soft context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s real Active job cost.

4. Retire zombie and superseded cost dumps before they travel

  • Superseded cost dumps (older estimate still looking live next to a real receipt, later actual, or revised hours) are labeled superseded or removed from the share view — they do not still inflate Active.
  • Walked-back, withdrawn, wrong-job, or already-booked rows are labeled stale or stay off — they do not still green the Active list.
  • When receipts, spreadsheet, and Slack disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier number because it fits the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material Active rows — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected merges, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Final skim: I can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older cost version, or something still needing confirmation.

A zombie superseded cost dump is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s current Active cost.

5. Keep Slack and reactions from rewriting the cost plan

  • Replace “looks fine” / “sounds good” folklore with the exact jobs, hours, materials, quantities, rates, actuals, billable flags, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the sheet.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite hours, actuals, Active/stale flags, or favor-as-Active rows.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the sheet you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this job, these hours, this material, this quantity, this actual, this superseded dump — instead of asking for another vague “looks fine.”
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the job-cost sheet look complete by guessing.
