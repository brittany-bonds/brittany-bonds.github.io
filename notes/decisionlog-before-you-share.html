The before-you-share checklist I use so a decision log still matches what was decided

A decision log can look handoff-ready while its meeting-notes story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the log leaves my hands.

A decision log can look handoff-ready while its story is already wrong: phantom decisions added so the Decisions column looks tidy; the same call from sticky + Slack + transcript counted three times; a silent scope upgrade greening “ship Friday” with no evidence; a brainstorm list still marked decided; action items mixed into Decisions so open work looks settled; a guessed “we basically agreed somehow” locked as fact; a superseded sticky still traveling next to the real call; open asks scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff; or Slack “looks fine” folklore treated as a confirmed decision list.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-pass folklore cleanup. This is the pass I run when the log is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every decided row as what was actually chosen in the room.

This complements earlier decisionlog work (first-pass meeting-notes-to-decision-log workflow and second-pass checklist so notes do not become Slack folklore). Those cover building and mid-pass folklore protection. This third pass is the pre-share evidence check — not a rewrite of either.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every decided row maps to a sticky/Slack/transcript/whiteboard crumb, meeting note, or a labeled ask — nothing invented for a tidy handoff preview.
  • Soft guesses (“we basically agreed somehow,” “sounds like ship Friday”) stay soft; hard decisions and owners only appear when a source supports them.
  • Same-call duplicates across sticky + Slack + transcript stay merged or labeled duplicate — they are not three open decisions.
  • Silent scope upgrades are walked back or evidenced — they are not left greening “ship X” with no crumb.
  • Brainstorm or parking-lot items still marked decided are flipped to brainstorm / deferred / ask — they do not still green this share’s decided list.
  • Action items and decisions stay separated — mixing open work into an unlabeled Decisions cell is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Guessed owners and “team will handle it” stay ask / owner TBD — they are not locked as decided fact because the column looked empty.
  • Superseded or zombie notes (older sticky still looking live next to the real call) are labeled superseded / stale or removed from the share view.
  • Open asks are listed once — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • A Slack “looks fine,” emoji reaction, or vague “sounds good” is not treated as a locked decision list unless the log states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean decision log,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final log and live evidence: the original meeting dump (notes app, Slack threads, Google Doc, whiteboard photo, voice memo, partial transcript, sticky “we agreed??” scraps), prior decision log if any, and the mid-pass folklore scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the log becomes someone else’s source of truth for what was decided.

1. Freeze decided rows to what was actually chosen

  • Every decided row maps to a source crumb, screenshot, transcript line, sticky, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “this is what we decided” look.
  • Phantom decisions, template filler, and “bonus” calls that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate decisions under two (or three) phrasings — sticky + Slack + transcript of the same call — are merged or marked duplicate — one choice is not three open slots because it looked tidy three times.
  • Decision wording stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same call or the same scope.
  • Action items stay out of Decisions — mixing them into one unlabeled “decided” cell is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each decided row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented decided row is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what was already chosen in the meeting.

2. Keep decision, owner, date, and status strength-honest

  • Soft notes (“we basically agreed somehow,” “sounds like Friday,” “probably Sam owns it”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard decisions, owners, or dates for neatness.
  • Hard decision / owner / date keep a transcript line, sticky, calendar note, or confirmer when available — silence is not consent to “ship X.”
  • Silent scope upgrades (row greened without a crumb — e.g. “MVP” quietly became “full launch”) are walked back to evidenced wording or labeled ask — they must not stay at a polished scope with no source.
  • Missing decision wording, owner, date, or status stays ask / ghost — a blank cell that looks decided is worse than a visible unknown.
  • Guessed owners and “about Friday somehow” dates stay labeled guess / ask — converting vibes into a tidy decided-this-week slice without a note is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Estimated or unclear rows stay marked estimated — inventing exact owners because the template looked empty is not cleanup.
  • Final skim: I can say which decided lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not project strategy or contract advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding what anyone must ship or own.

3. Separate decided from deferred, blocked, brainstorm, and open asks — list open asks once

  • Status vocabulary is named (decided / deferred / blocked / superseded / brainstorm / ask) — not blank cells that look settled.
  • Evidence for deferred, blocked, or superseded calls (note, archive flag, revisit cue) sits in notes or source — “I think we parked that” alone is not deferred.
  • Brainstorm or parking-lot items still marked decided are flipped off Decided — they do not still green this share’s decided list because the sticky is still in the dump.
  • Open questions stay out of Decisions filters until status says otherwise — burial in a thread is not a status.
  • Deferred items have a revisit cue once (date, next meeting, or “blocked on Y”) — folklore that “we’ll revisit somehow” is not a rule.
  • Open asks (missing owner, unclear wording, which phrasing is canonical, who confirmed, conflicting sticky vs transcript) are listed once in a visible place — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • Final skim: I can say what is decided, what is deferred/blocked, what is brainstorm/parked, and what still needs confirmation without reopening Slack.

A brainstorm ghost can be useful archive context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s decided call.

4. Retire zombie and stale note versions before they travel

  • Superseded notes (older sticky still looking live next to the real call, or an outdated transcript excerpt next to a later Slack confirm) are labeled superseded or removed from the share view — they do not still inflate Decided.
  • Walked-back, withdrawn, out-of-meeting, or wrong-thread rows are labeled stale or stay off — they do not still green the decided list.
  • When sticky, Slack, and transcript disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier decision or owner because it fits the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material decided rows — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected options, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Final skim: I can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older note version, or something still needing confirmation.

A zombie superseded note is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s decided call.

5. Keep Slack and reactions from rewriting the decided list

  • Replace “looks fine” / “sounds good” folklore with the exact decisions, owners, dates, statuses, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the log.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite decisions, owners, dates, or deferred/decided flags.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the log you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this decision wording, this owner, this date, this deferred flag, this superseded note — instead of asking for another vague “looks fine.”
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the decision log look complete by guessing.
