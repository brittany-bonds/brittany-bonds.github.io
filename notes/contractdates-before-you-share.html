The before-you-share checklist I use so a key-dates one-pager still matches the contract

A key-dates one-pager can look calendar-ready while its date story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the page leaves my hands.

A key-dates one-pager can look calendar-ready while its story is already wrong: a milestone invented because the client “probably meant Friday”; a soft range quietly upgraded to a hard start; a notice window computed from the wrong side of the table; an amendment PDF left out of the share view; a Slack “looks fine” rewriting act-by dates; or a superseded SOW still shipping as this period’s dates.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-dates cleanup. This is the pass I run when the one-pager is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every line as what the signed paper actually says.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every date row maps to a clause, page, exhibit, amendment, or a labeled ask — nothing invented for a tidy calendar preview.
  • Soft ranges stay soft; hard act-by dates only appear when a source supports them.
  • Notice windows show whose clock and which direction (provider vs client) — not a generic “30 days” with no act-by.
  • Slack guesses and “looks fine” stay labeled guess / ask — not counsel-confirmed.
  • Superseded SOWs or out-of-window amendments are labeled stale or removed from the share view.
  • An emoji reaction or vague “lgtm” is not treated as a locked calendar unless the page states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean dates page,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final one-pager and live evidence: the signed (or near-final) PDF/DOCX, amendments and side-letters, prior date page if any, and the mid-dates scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the page becomes someone else’s calendar source of truth.

1. Freeze date rows to what the paper says

  • Every date row maps to a clause, page, exhibit, amendment, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “this is the calendar” look.
  • Invented milestones, template filler, and “bonus” deadlines that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate phrasings of the same deadline are merged or marked duplicate — one act-by is not two calendar slots because it looked tidy twice.
  • Labeling stays close to the source wording; a polished synonym may not be the same obligation.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented date is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what the signed paper already requires.

2. Keep date strength honest

  • Soft notes (“around mid-month,” “roughly 30 days”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard act-by dates for neatness.
  • Timezone and business-day rules stay labeled when they affect the act-by; a pretty local date is not a silent rewrite of the clause.
  • Open ranges stay open or labeled ask — they are not narrowed to a single Friday because that looked decisive.
  • Final skim: I can say which dates are hard, which are soft, and which still need a clause check.

3. Keep notice windows side-honest

  • Each notice window names whose clock and which direction — provider vs client is not interchangeable polish.
  • “30 days” alone is incomplete; the share view shows an act-by date or a labeled ask for how to compute it.
  • Amendments that change notice stay in the share view or flagged stale base when the base SOW alone would mislead.
  • Final skim: I can answer “by when, for whom, from which document?” for every notice row.

4. Keep Slack and reactions from rewriting the calendar

  • Replace “they said looks fine” with the exact dates, notice windows, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the page.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite act-by dates.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the page you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this start, this notice act-by, this amendment — instead of asking for another vague thumbs-up.
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the key-dates one-pager look complete by guessing.
