The before-you-share checklist I use so a case-study one-pager still matches the testimonials

A case-study one-pager can look finished while its proof story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the page leaves my hands.

A case-study one-pager can look finished while its proof story is already wrong: a quote nobody said sits in the page for polish; soft praise is dressed as a hard metric; two conflicting outcomes share one tidy win; a Slack guess is greened as a confirmed result; a superseded testimonial is still shipping; or an emoji “sounds good” is doing all the approving with no matching quote or outcome.

Before-you-share is different from mid-story cleanup. This is the pass I run when the one-pager is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every line as what the client actually said and locked.

This complements earlier case-study work (first-pass testimonial-dump-to-one-pager workflow and mid-story honesty cleanup). Those cover building and mid-pass cleanup. This third pass is the pre-share evidence check — not a rewrite of either.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every quote maps to something in the dump (or a labeled ask) — nothing invented for completeness.
  • Soft praise stays soft; hard metric language only appears when a source supports it.
  • Conflicting outcomes stay labeled conflict / ask — they are not averaged into one green win.
  • Slack guesses and “I think they meant…” stay labeled guess / ask — not client-confirmed.
  • Superseded or replaced testimonials are labeled stale or removed from the share view.
  • An emoji reaction or vague “sounds good” is not treated as locked proof unless the page states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean case study,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final one-pager and live evidence: the original testimonial dump, email/Slack exports, review screenshots, Loom transcripts, survey replies, prior one-pager if any, and the mid-story scratch trail. The goal is not to make every line complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the page becomes someone else’s source of truth about what the client said.

1. Freeze quotes to what was said

  • Every quote maps to a dump line, screenshot, transcript, or a labeled ask — no orphan line gets a confident “they said this” look.
  • Invented quotes, template filler, and “bonus” praise that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate praise under two phrasings (email + screenshot of the same line) is merged or marked duplicate — one compliment is not two proof blocks because it looked tidy twice.
  • Attribution wording stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same speaker or project.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each quote back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented quote is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what the client already said.

2. Keep praise and outcome strength-honest

  • Soft praise (“great work,” “loved working with you”) stays soft — it is not upgraded to a hard metric for neatness.
  • Hard outcomes keep a source, date, or named confirmer when available — silence is not consent.
  • Metrics, timelines, and before/after numbers stay tied to a source or labeled guess / TBD / ask.
  • Competing outcomes stay side by side as conflict / ask — do not average two figures into one green win.
  • Result language matches the latest source; a polished synonym may not be the same change.
  • Skim test: a reader can tell which lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not marketing advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding what anyone must claim or publish.

3. Retire superseded testimonials before they travel

  • Walked-back, replaced, or older testimonials are labeled stale or removed from the share view — they do not still green a proof cell.
  • When email, Slack, and review screenshots disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier win because it fits the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material quotes and outcomes — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected options, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Skim test: a reader can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older testimonial, or something still needing confirmation.

A superseded testimonial is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as today’s share-ready proof.

4. Challenge the Slack “sounds good”

  • Replace “they said sounds good” with the exact points that were confirmed, open, or never named on the one-pager.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite quotes or outcome totals.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the page you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this quote, this metric, this conflict — instead of asking for another vague thumbs-up.
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the case study look complete by guessing.
