The before-you-share checklist I use so a lead tracker still matches what is live

A lead tracker can look chase-ready while its inquiry-dump story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the sheet leaves my hands.

A lead tracker can look chase-ready while its story is already wrong: a phantom lead added for a tidy open count; the same person from email + form + DM counted three times; a silent status upgrade greening “qualified” with no evidence; a closed-won or closed-lost ghost still sitting in Open; a guessed “ping next week somehow” locked as next-step fact; a superseded sticky note still traveling next to the real ask; open asks scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff; or Slack “follow up later” folklore treated as a confirmed chase list.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-pipeline cleanup. This is the pass I run when the sheet is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every open row as what is still live to chase.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every open row maps to an email/DM/form crumb, sticky note, CRM screenshot, or a labeled ask — nothing invented for a tidy chase-list preview.
  • Soft guesses (“ping next week somehow,” “probably hot”) stay soft; hard status and next-action dates only appear when a source supports them.
  • Same-person duplicates across email + form + DM stay merged or labeled duplicate — they are not three open leads.
  • Silent status upgrades are walked back or evidenced — they are not left greening “qualified / proposal” with no crumb.
  • Closed-won / closed-lost / “not a fit” ghosts are flipped off Open — they do not still green this week’s chase slice.
  • Guessed next-steps stay ask / guess — they are not locked as chase fact because the column looked empty.
  • Superseded or zombie notes (older sticky still looking live next to the real ask) are labeled superseded / stale or removed from the share view.
  • Open asks are listed once — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • A Slack “follow up later,” emoji reaction, or vague “looks fine” is not treated as a locked chase list unless the sheet states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean lead tracker,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final sheet and live evidence: the original inquiry dump (email folder, DM export, form CSV, CRM screenshot, sticky notes, Slack “follow up later” pings), prior open-lead sheet if any, and the mid-pipeline scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the sheet becomes someone else’s chase-list source of truth.

1. Freeze open rows to what is still live

  • Every open row maps to a source crumb, screenshot, export, sticky, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “this is the chase list” look.
  • Phantom leads, template filler, and “bonus” contacts that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate inquiries under two (or three) phrasings — email + form + DM of the same person + ask — are merged or marked duplicate — one inquiry is not three open slots because it looked tidy three times.
  • Name/spelling stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same person or the same ask.
  • Tire-kickers and clearly out-of-window noise stay parked or archived — mixing them into this week’s Open is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each open row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented open row is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what is already live for this chase window.

2. Keep status, next step, and date strength-honest

  • Soft notes (“ping next week somehow,” “probably hot,” “usual nurture”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard status or next-action dates for neatness.
  • Hard status keeps an email reply, call note, form field, date, or confirmer when available — silence is not consent to “qualified.”
  • Silent status upgrades (row greened without a crumb) are walked back to evidenced stage or labeled ask — they must not stay at a polished stage with no source.
  • Missing next action or next-action date stays ask / ghost — a blank cell that looks chase-ready is worse than a visible unknown.
  • Guessed close dates and “about Friday somehow” stay labeled guess / ask — converting vibes into a tidy due-this-week slice without a note is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Ask and primary contact stay labeled; inventing a handle or one-line ask because the template looked empty is not cleanup.
  • Final skim: I can say which open lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not sales strategy or CRM advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding who anyone must pitch or when.

3. Separate chase-ready from closed/won ghosts, “not a fit,” and nurture parking — list open asks once

  • Status vocabulary is named (new / contacted / qualified / proposal / won / lost / nurture / ask) — not blank cells that look chase-ready.
  • Evidence for won / lost / “not a fit” (reply, note, archive flag) sits in notes or source — “I think we closed that” alone is not closed.
  • Closed-won, closed-lost, and “not a fit” rows are flipped off Open — they do not still green this week’s chase slice because the sticky is still in the dump.
  • Nurture / parked rows stay out of due-this-week filters until status says otherwise — burial in a thread is not a status.
  • Owner (who follows up on their side) is named or unowned — folklore that “they’ll follow up later” is not ownership.
  • Open asks (missing contact, unclear ask, which spelling is canonical, who owns follow-up, conflicting email vs form) are listed once in a visible place — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • Final skim: I can say what is chase-ready, what is won/lost/not-a-fit, what is nurture/parked, and what still needs confirmation without reopening Slack.

A closed/won ghost can be useful archive context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this window’s share-ready open pipeline.

4. Retire zombie and stale note versions before they travel

  • Superseded notes (older sticky still looking live next to the real ask, or an outdated CRM screenshot next to a later reply) are labeled superseded or removed from the share view — they do not still inflate Open.
  • Walked-back, withdrawn, out-of-window, or wrong-person rows are labeled stale or stay off — they do not still green the chase list.
  • When email, form, and DM disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier status or next step because it fits the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material open rows — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected options, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Final skim: I can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older note version, or something still needing confirmation.

A zombie superseded note is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this window’s share-ready open lead.

5. Keep Slack and reactions from rewriting the chase list

  • Replace “follow up later” / “looks fine” folklore with the exact people, asks, statuses, next actions, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the sheet.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite status, next-action dates, or closed/won flags.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the sheet you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this person, this ask, this status, this next action, this closed/won flag, this superseded note — instead of asking for another vague “follow up later.”
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the lead tracker look complete by guessing.
