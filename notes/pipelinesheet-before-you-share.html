The before-you-share checklist I use so an open-quotes pipeline sheet still matches what is live

A sales pipeline sheet can look chase-ready while its open-quotes story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the sheet leaves my hands.

A pipeline sheet can look chase-ready while its story is already wrong: a zombie quote still greening the active tab past the stale rule; a Slack “looks good” quietly upgraded to Closed-won with no signature or deposit; FINAL and FINAL_v7 both counting as two open deals; currency fog summed as one total; “the client” still listed as owner; or a sticky titled “ping Friday somehow” rewritten as this week’s chase plan.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-pipeline cleanup. This is the pass I run when the sheet is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every row as what is actually still open.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every still-open quote is one row — twin PDFs of the same deal are merged or labeled duplicate, not two silent forecasts.
  • Stage is evidenced or labeled ask / guess / TBD — soft “looks good” Slack is not Closed-won.
  • Amount has currency and tax-in/out or is labeled unknown — no invented round number for a tidy total.
  • Next action has a named owner and a dated due or is clearly open — “ping somehow” is not a plan.
  • Stale / closed-won / closed-lost / superseded are parked once — not still greening the active view.
  • Nothing invented beyond what the dump supports — guessed verbal closes and vibe deposits stay labeled.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean pipeline sheet,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final pipeline sheet and live evidence: the quote PDFs/DOCX, email or Slack threads, Drive links, prior sheet if any, and the mid-pipeline scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the sheet becomes someone else’s chase list.

1. Freeze open rows to what the dump still supports

  • Every open row maps to a quote file, thread, send date, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “this is still live” look.
  • Expired, won, lost, withdrawn, and superseded quotes are off the active share view or clearly labeled — polish is not permission to keep them greening.
  • Twin hits of the same deal (FINAL vs FINAL_v7, PDF + email forward) are merged or marked duplicate — one opportunity is not two chase slots.
  • Soft scope is named (this offer line / this book of business) — other clients’ PDFs stay out or flagged.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each active row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented next action is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what the dump already agreed to chase.

2. Keep stage and amount honest

  • Soft notes (“looks good,” emoji reacts, “basically yes”) stay Verbal or Negotiating + ask — they are not upgraded to Closed-won without signature or deposit evidence.
  • Amounts keep currency and tax-in/out labels when they affect the total; a pretty round number is not a silent rewrite of the quote.
  • Open or missing amounts stay open or labeled ask — they are not filled with “about $5k” because the tab looked thin.
  • Currency mismatches across rows are flagged once — do not silently sum USD + EUR as one forecast total.
  • Final skim: I can say which stages are evidenced, which amounts are locked, and which still need a source check.

3. Keep owners and next touches real

  • Each next action names a real person (you or someone on their side) — “the client” alone is incomplete.
  • “Ping Friday somehow” alone is incomplete; the share view shows a dated due or a labeled ask for who acts when.
  • Last touch is dated when it matters; undated “we talked” folklore stays labeled needs confirmation.
  • Ghost stickies without a sheet row are parked or turned into real rows — they do not become the share view alone.
  • Final skim: I can answer “who acts, by when, from which quote?” for every chase-ready row.

4. Keep Slack and reactions from rewriting the pipeline

  • Replace “they said looks fine” with the exact stages, amounts, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the sheet.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite Closed-won or invent a deposit.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the sheet you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this stage, this amount/currency, this next touch — instead of asking for another vague thumbs-up.
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of what is still open.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this row, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the pipeline sheet look complete by guessing.
