The before-you-share checklist I use so a kickoff one-pager still matches the notes

A kickoff one-pager can look share-ready while its story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the page leaves my hands.

A kickoff one-pager can look share-ready while its story is already wrong: a phantom goal is added for a tidy “aligned” paragraph; a silent scope upgrade greened “we’re also doing X” with no signed crumb; a superseded sticky or withdrawn “maybe we also” still travels next to the real deliverable; open asks are scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff; or Slack “looks good” folklore is treated as locked start-of-project truth.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-kickoff cleanup. This is the pass I run when the one-pager is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every goal, deliverable, owner, and date as what was actually agreed.

I am not a CPA, bookkeeper, lawyer, project manager, or tax advisor, and this is not financial, tax, legal, or contractual advice. It is an administrative proof-checking routine, not a recommendation about what anyone should build, sign, price, or commit to.

This complements earlier kickoff notes-to-one-pager work (first-pass workflow, week-one scope-drift checklist, and mid-kickoff honesty cleanup). Those cover building and mid-pass cleanup. This third pass is the pre-share evidence check — not a rewrite of any of them.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every goal, deliverable, owner, and date maps to a kickoff crumb (call notes, transcript, email, Notion dump, whiteboard photo, signed brief/SOW) or a labeled ask — nothing invented for a tidy start preview.
  • Soft guesses (“probably Friday somehow,” “the team owns it”) stay soft; hard dates and owners only appear when a source supports them.
  • Silent scope upgrades (“we’re also doing X”) are walked back or evidenced — they are not left greening the one-pager with no crumb.
  • Phantom goals and template filler that never appeared in the notes stay out or labeled proposed / ask.
  • Superseded or zombie notes (older sticky still looking live next to the real ask) are labeled superseded / stale or removed from the share view.
  • Open asks are listed once — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • A Slack “looks good,” emoji reaction, or vague thumbs-up is not treated as locked scope unless the page states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean kickoff page,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final one-pager and live evidence: the original kickoff dump (call notes, transcript, email chain, Notion page, whiteboard photo, Slack “looks good” pings), any signed brief/SOW, and the mid-kickoff scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the page becomes someone else’s start-of-project source of truth.

1. Freeze goals and deliverables to what was actually recorded

  • Every goal and deliverable maps to a source crumb, screenshot, export, sticky, signed line, or a labeled ask — no orphan line gets a confident “this is what we agreed” look.
  • Phantom goals, template filler, and “bonus” outcomes that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate asks under two phrasings — Slack + email + Notion of the same outcome — are merged or marked duplicate — one ask is not three locked deliverables.
  • Wording stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same goal or the same out-of-scope line.
  • Tire-kicker “maybe someday” noise stays parked or archived — mixing it into this start page is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each goal/deliverable back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented goal is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what was already agreed for project start.

2. Keep owners, dates, and success criteria strength-honest

  • Soft notes (“probably Friday somehow,” “usual review cycle,” “the team”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard owners or dates for neatness.
  • Hard owners keep a real name when available — “the team,” an emoji reaction, or silence is not ownership.
  • Silent scope upgrades (page greened with “we’re also doing X” without a crumb) are walked back to evidenced scope or labeled ask.
  • Missing start / draft / review / go-live dates stay ask / ghost — a blank cell that looks scheduled is worse than a visible unknown.
  • Guessed success criteria and “about good enough somehow” stay labeled guess / ask — converting vibes into a tidy done-definition without a note is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Non-goals stay explicit when stated; inventing a tidy out-of-scope list because the template looked empty is not cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can tell which lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not project-management or contractual advice. I am recording what the kickoff dump disclosed, not deciding what anyone must build or when.

3. Separate agreed start truth from unsigned maybes — and list open asks once

  • Status vocabulary is named (agreed / proposed / ask / out of scope / superseded) — not blank cells that look locked.
  • Evidence for agreed vs proposed (signed line, confirmer, date) sits in notes or source — “I think we locked that” alone is not agreed.
  • Unsigned “maybe we also…” piles are parked as opens — they do not still green the deliverables list.
  • Non-goals that quietly became goals are walked back or evidenced — folklore is not scope.
  • Owner (who decides / who delivers on their side) is named or unowned — folklore that “they’ll figure it out” is not ownership.
  • Open asks (missing owner, unclear non-goal, which wording is canonical, conflicting Slack vs email) are listed once in a visible place — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • Skim test: a reader can say what is agreed, what is proposed, what is out of scope, and what still needs confirmation without reopening Slack.

An unsigned maybe can be useful parking. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this window’s share-ready start truth.

4. Retire zombie and stale note versions before they travel

  • Superseded notes (older sticky still looking live next to the real ask, or an outdated Notion dump next to a later reply) are labeled superseded or removed from the share view — they do not still inflate the start page.
  • Walked-back, withdrawn, out-of-window, or wrong-thread rows are labeled stale or stay-off — they do not still green goals or deliverables.
  • When Slack, email, and Notion disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the prettier scope because it fits the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material lines — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected options, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Skim test: a reader can tell whether each line describes the current kickoff dump, an older note version, or something still needing confirmation.

A zombie superseded note is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this window’s share-ready project start.

5. Challenge the Slack “looks good”

  • Replace “looks good” / “lgtm” folklore with the exact goals, deliverables, owners, dates, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the page.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite scope, owners, dates, or out-of-scope flags.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the page you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this goal, this deliverable, this owner, this date, this superseded note — instead of asking for another vague “looks good.”
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the kickoff one-pager look complete by guessing.
