The before-you-share checklist I use so a follow-up tracker still matches the chase notes

A follow-up tracker can look Monday-ready while its chase story is already wrong. This is the pre-share evidence check I run before the sheet leaves my hands.

A follow-up tracker can look Monday-ready while its story is already wrong: phantom chase rows invented so the Active column looks busy; the same chase counted twice across email + Slack + CRM; a silent status upgrade greening “emailed” into “promised payment” with no crumb; a zombie superseded chase note still looking live next to a real reply; open asks scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff; Slack “looks fine” / emoji folklore treated as a locked chase plan; or a favor / one-off “I’ll ping them sometime” still marked Active as a real next step.

Before-you-share is different from a mid-chase honesty cleanup. This is the pass I run when the tracker is about to leave my hands and someone else may treat every Active row as what the chase notes actually supported.

This complements earlier followupsheet work (first-pass chase-notes-to-follow-up-tracker workflow and second-pass mid-chase honesty checklist). Those cover building and mid-pass honesty protection. This third pass is the pre-share evidence check — not a rewrite of either.

What “honest enough to share” looks like

  • Every Active chase maps to a sticky, Slack DM, email draft, CRM leftover, calendar ghost, or a labeled ask — nothing invented for a tidy handoff preview.
  • Soft guesses (“I’ll ping them sometime,” “probably overdue”) stay soft; hard next actions and due dates only appear when a source supports them or the owner explicitly chose them.
  • Same-chase duplicates across email + Slack + CRM stay merged or labeled duplicate — they are not three open nudges.
  • Silent status upgrades (“emailed” greened to “promised payment” with no crumb) are walked back or evidenced — they are not left polishing a chase with no reply.
  • Favor, soft check-in, or one-off “I’ll ping them sometime” rows still marked Active as a real next step are flipped to Soft / Parked — they do not still green this share’s Active list.
  • Stake stays labeled when it is money or a blocked handoff — folding soft social into the money pile without a note is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Guessed “ASAP” and “urgent somehow” numbers stay ask / fake urgency — they are not locked as fact because the column looked empty.
  • Superseded or zombie chase notes (older “circle back” still looking live next to a real reply or paid invoice) are labeled superseded / stale or removed from the share view.
  • Open asks are listed once — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • A Slack “looks fine,” emoji reaction, or vague “sounds good” is not treated as a locked chase plan unless the tracker states what was confirmed.

If the only share instruction is “send the clean follow-up tracker,” pre-share honesty has already slipped.

The before-you-send checklist

Use this against the near-final tracker and live evidence: the original chase dump (stickies, Slack “circle back” DMs, email drafts, CRM leftovers, calendar ghosts, Notion half-checkboxes), prior tracker if any, and the mid-chase honesty scratch trail. The goal is not to make every cell complete. The goal is to make uncertainty visible before the tracker becomes someone else’s chase plan for what to nudge.

1. Freeze Active chases to what the notes actually supported

  • Every Active row maps to a source crumb, sticky, Slack DM, email draft, CRM leftover, or a labeled ask — no orphan row gets a confident “nudge this today” look.
  • Phantom chase rows, template filler, and “bonus” contacts that never appeared in the dump stay out or labeled proposed / ask — polish is not permission.
  • Duplicate chases under two (or three) phrasings — email + Slack + CRM of the same invoice or decision — are merged or marked duplicate — one chase is not three open slots because it looked tidy three times.
  • Contact and item wording stays close to the source; a polished synonym may not be the same person or the same stake.
  • Draft estimates, internal scratch, and angry unsent drafts stay out of Active — mixing them into one unlabeled “next nudge” cell is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Skim test: a reader can point from each Active row back to a source, or see that it still needs confirmation.

An invented Active chase is not automatically useless. It is a proposal. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as what the chase notes already supported.

2. Keep status, last touch, next action, and due strength-honest

  • Soft notes (“I’ll ping them sometime,” “probably still open,” “sounds overdue”) stay soft — they are not upgraded to hard next actions or due dates for neatness.
  • Hard status / last touch / next action / due keep a crumb or confirmer when available — silence is not consent to a new chase plan.
  • Silent status upgrades (row greened from “emailed” to “promised payment” without a reply crumb) are walked back to evidenced wording or labeled ask — they must not stay at a polished status with no source.
  • Missing last touch, next action, due, channel, or stake stays ask / ghost — a blank cell that looks nudge-ready is worse than a visible unknown.
  • Last touch and next action stay separate — overwriting history so the tracker looks “clean” is a rewrite, not a cleanup.
  • Estimated or unclear rows stay marked estimated — inventing exact due dates because the template looked empty is not cleanup.
  • Final skim: I can say which Active lines are locked, which are soft, and which still need confirmation.

This is a consistency check, not collections strategy or payment advice. I am recording what the dump disclosed, not deciding who anyone must chase next.

3. Separate Active from stale, soft/favor, waiting-on-me, and open asks — list open asks once

  • Status vocabulary is named (active / waiting on them / waiting on me / stale / soft / done / cancelled / ask) — not blank cells that look settled.
  • Evidence for stale, soft, or parked chases (note, archive flag, Soft / History section) sits in notes or source — “I think we stopped chasing that” alone is not closed.
  • Favor, soft check-in, or one-off “I’ll ping them sometime” rows still marked Active as a real next step are flipped off Active — they do not still green this share’s nudge list because the sticky is still in the dump.
  • Money vs soft social stays labeled — burial of stake in a notes cell is not a status.
  • Fake urgency (“ASAP” with no client deadline) stays flagged — mixing it into true overdue without a note is folklore, not a chase.
  • Open asks (missing last touch, unclear next action, which channel is canonical, conflict between sticky and Slack reply, confidential strip needed) are listed once in a visible place — not scattered as footnotes nobody will find at handoff.
  • Final skim: I can say what is Active, what is stale/soft/parked, and what still needs confirmation without reopening Slack.

A soft “ping sometime” ghost can be useful Soft context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s real next step.

4. Retire zombie and superseded chase notes before they travel

  • Superseded chase notes (older “circle back” still looking live next to a real reply, paid invoice, or later status) are labeled superseded or removed from the share view — they do not still inflate Active.
  • Walked-back, withdrawn, wrong-contact, or already-cleared rows are labeled stale or stay off — they do not still green the Active list.
  • When email, Slack, and CRM disagree, keep the conflict visible as recheck — do not pick the easier status because it fits the template.
  • Capture or last-checked date exists for material Active rows — “I cleaned this last week” is not a freshness rule.
  • Raw scratch, rejected merges, and unresolved conflicts stay in the evidence trail but out of the clean share view.
  • Final skim: I can tell whether each line describes the current dump, an older chase version, or something still needing confirmation.

A zombie superseded chase note is historical context. The misleading part is leaving it dressed as this share’s current next nudge.

5. Keep Slack and reactions from rewriting the chase plan

  • Replace “looks fine” / “sounds good” folklore with the exact contacts, items, statuses, next actions, dues, channels, stakes, or open asks that were confirmed, parked, or never named on the tracker.
  • An emoji reaction, “lgtm,” or vague thumbs-up is labeled by what it covered — it does not silently rewrite status, next action, Active/stale flags, or soft-as-Active rows.
  • If Slack approved a different version than the tracker you are about to send, stop and reconcile — do not ship the prettier draft under the older reaction.
  • Make the share ask specific — confirm this contact, this item, this status, this next action, this due, this superseded note — instead of asking for another vague “looks fine.”
  • Leave a visible needs confirmation marker where sharing would otherwise create a false record of agreement.
  • Final skim: I can answer “what source supports this line, what still needs confirmation, and what did Slack actually cover?” for every share-ready row.

If a row fails one of these checks, I would rather send a visible needs confirmation label than make the follow-up tracker look complete by guessing.
